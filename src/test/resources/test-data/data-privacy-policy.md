## 1. Purpose & Scope

This policy establishes TechGlobal Solutions Inc. ("Company," "we," "us," "our") commitment to protecting personal data and ensuring compliance with global data protection regulations. It applies to all processing of personal data by the Company, whether as Controller or Processor, across all jurisdictions where we operate.

**Scope includes:**

- All employees, contractors, third parties with access to personal data
- All systems, applications, databases, paper records, backups
- All processing activities: collection, storage, use, disclosure, transfer, deletion
- Customer data, employee data, candidate data, vendor data, website visitors

## 2. Data Protection Principles (GDPR Art. 5)

### 2.1 Lawfulness, Fairness & Transparency

- Legal basis identified for every processing activity (Art. 6 GDPR)
- Privacy notices provided at collection (Art. 13/14)
- No processing beyond disclosed purposes without new notice/consent

### 2.2 Purpose Limitation

- Data collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes
- Further processing compatible only if: archiving, research, statistics (with safeguards)

### 2.3 Data Minimization

- Adequate, relevant, limited to what is necessary
- Regular reviews to purge unnecessary data fields

### 2.4 Accuracy

- Reasonable steps to ensure accuracy; rectification without delay
- Data subjects can update via self-service portals

### 2.5 Storage Limitation

- Retained only as long as necessary per Records Retention Schedule
- Automated deletion/anonymization where feasible

### 2.6 Integrity & Confidentiality (Security)

- Technical & organizational measures per Section 8
- Encryption, pseudonymization, access controls, monitoring

### 2.7 Accountability

- DPO appointed; records of processing activities (ROPA) maintained
- DPIAs for high-risk processing; vendor DPAs; breach procedures

## 3. Legal Bases for Processing

### 3.1 Customer Data

- Contract performance (Art. 6(1)(b)): Service delivery, billing, support
- Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)): Product improvement, security, fraud prevention
- Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)): Marketing, analytics cookies, optional features
- Legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)): Tax, audit, regulatory reporting

### 3.2 Employee & Candidate Data

- Contract/pre-contract (Art. 6(1)(b)): Payroll, benefits, background checks
- Legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)): Tax, immigration, safety, EEO reporting
- Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)): Workforce analytics, security monitoring
- Consent: Voluntary programs (wellness, ERGs, photo directory)
- Special category (Art. 9): Health (benefits, accommodations), biometrics (access), union membership

### 3.3 Vendor/Partner Data

- Contract (Art. 6(1)(b)): Procurement, payments, performance management
- Legitimate interest: Vendor risk management, due diligence

4. Coordinate: Data owners across systems (Workday, Salesforce, Snowflake, etc.)

## 5. Consent Management

### 5.1 Consent Standards

- Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous (GDPR Art. 4(11))
- Granular: Separate consent for each purpose (marketing, analytics, profiling)
- Easy withdrawal: Same mechanism as giving (one-click unsubscribe)
- No pre-ticked boxes; no bundling with T&Cs

### 5.2 Consent Records

- Stored: Timestamp, version of notice, mechanism, IP address, user agent
- Retained: Duration of processing + 3 years post-withdrawal
- Auditable: Consent dashboard for data subjects

### 5.3 Cookie Consent

- Categories: Strictly Necessary, Functional, Analytics, Advertising
- Banner: Geo-aware (GDPR/CCPA/LGPD variants)
- TCF 2.2 compliance for programmatic advertising

## 6. Cross-Border Data Transfers

### 6.1 Transfer Mechanisms (Priority Order)

1. Adequacy Decision: EU→US (DPF), UK, Canada, Japan, Israel, NZ, Switzerland, Uruguay, Argentina, Andorra, Faroe Islands, Guernsey, Isle of Man, Jersey
2. Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs): 2021 EU SCCs + UK Addendum
3. Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs): In progress (target 2025)
4. Derogations: Explicit consent, contract necessity, vital interests (exceptional)

### 6.2 Transfer Impact Assessments (TIAs)

- Required for all SCC transfers to non-adequate countries
- Assess: Laws of destination, supplementary measures, importer obligations
- Documented in ROPA; reviewed annually or on legal change

### 6.3 Restricted Jurisdictions

- China (PIPL): Local storage required for critical data; security assessment for export
- Russia: Localization law (152-FZ) — Russian citizen data stored in RF
- US: DPF certification for US entities; SCCs + supplementary measures for others

## 7. Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)

### 7.1 When Required (GDPR Art. 35)

- Systematic profiling with significant effects
- Large-scale special category data processing
- Large-scale public monitoring
- New technologies (AI/ML, biometrics, IoT)
- High-risk per EDPB guidelines (WP248)

### 7.2 DPIA Process

1. Screening: Project intake form → Privacy team determines need
2. Assessment: DPIA template (description, necessity, risks, measures)
3. Consultation: DPO + Security + Legal + Business Owner
4. Mitigation: Technical/organizational measures with owners/dates
5. Sign-off: DPO approval; high residual risk → Supervisory Authority consultation
6. Monitor: Annual review or on material change

### 7.3 AI/ML Specific DPIAs

- Training data provenance, bias testing, explainability, human review
- Model cards documented; retraining triggers re-assessment

## 8. Technical & Organizational Measures (TOMs)

### 8.1 Access Control

- Role-based access (RBAC) with least privilege
- Quarterly access reviews (managers + data owners)
- Privileged access: PAM (CyberArk), session recording, break-glass
- MFA for all personal data access

### 8.2 Encryption

- At rest: AES-256 (databases, files, backups, laptops)
- In transit: TLS 1.2+ (mutual TLS for service-to-service)
- Key management: AWS KMS / Azure Key Vault / HashiCorp Vault (HSM-backed)
- Email: Office 365 Message Encryption for CONFIDENTIAL/RESTRICTED

### 8.3 Pseudonymization & Anonymization

- Tokenization: Production → Non-prod (Format-preserving encryption)
- Synthetic data: Generated for ML training (Gretel/Mostly AI)
- K-anonymity (k≥5), l-diversity, t-closeness for analytics datasets

### 8.4 Data Loss Prevention (DLP)

- Network: Forcepoint/Zscaler (email, web, cloud)
- Endpoint: Microsoft Purview / CrowdStrike
- Classification: Regex, dictionary, ML classifiers (PII, PHI, PCI, secrets)
- Actions: Block, encrypt, quarantine, alert, user justification

### 8.5 Logging & Monitoring

- Personal data access logged (who, what, when, why)
- SIEM correlation: Splunk + Chronicle
- Anomaly detection: UEBA for excessive access, off-hours, geo-impossible
- Retention: 13 months hot / 7 years cold

## 9. Vendor & Third-Party Management

### 9.1 Data Processing Agreements (DPAs)

- Required for ALL processors (Art. 28 GDPR)
- Standard DPA template (Company favorable); negotiations tracked
- Sub-processors: Prior written authorization; flow-down terms
- International transfers: SCCs embedded in DPA

### 9.2 Vendor Privacy Assessment

- Tier 1 (High): Access to personal data → Full privacy assessment + DPA + annual review
- Tier 2 (Medium): Limited access → Abbreviated assessment + DPA
- Tier 3 (Low): No personal data → Standard contract clauses only
- Assessment: Security questionnaire, certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), breach history

### 9.3 Onboarding & Offboarding

- Onboarding: Privacy review → DPA execution → Access provisioning → Monitoring
- Offboarding: Data return/destruction certification → Access revocation → Audit

## 11. Data Retention & Disposal

### 11.1 Retention Principles

- Legal requirement > Contractual > Business need > Consent
- Minimum necessary period; anonymize when identification no longer needed
- Legal hold overrides retention schedule

### 11.2 Key Retention Periods

| Customer contracts | 7 years post-termination | Contract law, SOX |
| Employee records | 7 years post-separation | FLSA, Title VII, ADEA |
| Payroll/tax | 7 years | IRS, SSA |

### 11.3 Disposal Methods

- Digital: Cryptographic erasure (key destruction) + verification
- Physical: Cross-cut shred (NIST 800-88), pulping, incineration
- Certification: Destruction certificates for all media

## 12. Data Breach Management

### 12.1 Breach Definition

- Confidentiality: Unauthorized access/disclosure
- Integrity: Unauthorized alteration
- Availability: Accidental loss/destruction (ransomware, outage > 4 hrs)

### 12.2 Notification Timelines

- Supervisory Authority: 72 hours from awareness (GDPR Art. 33)
- Data Subjects: Without undue delay if high risk (Art. 34)
- CCPA: "In the most expedient time possible" (practical: 72 hours)
- Regulators: Sector-specific (HIPAA 60 days, NYDFS 72 hours, etc.)

### 12.3 Breach Response Process

1. Detect & Contain (0-4 hrs): IR team, isolate systems, preserve evidence
2. Assess (4-24 hrs): Scope, data types, subjects affected, risk level
3. Notify (24-72 hrs): DPO → Legal → Executive → Regulators → Subjects
4. Remediate: Root cause, patches, enhanced monitoring, user comms
5. Lessons Learned: Post-mortem within 14 days; improvements tracked

### 12.4 Breach Register

- All incidents logged (including near-misses)
- Fields: Date, description, categories, subjects, risk, notifications, status
- Annual summary to Board Audit Committee

## 13. Privacy by Design & Default

### 13.1 System Development Lifecycle (SDLC)

- Privacy requirements in Epic/Feature definition
- Threat modeling: STRIDE + LINDDUN (privacy-specific)
- Code review: Privacy checklist (data minimization, encryption, logging)
- Pre-production: DPIA gate for high-risk features
- Production: Privacy regression tests in CI/CD

### 13.2 Default Settings

- Opt-out by default for non-essential processing
- Minimal data collection (progressive profiling)
- Shortest retention viable
- Access logs enabled by default

## 14. Children's Data

### 14.2 Special Protections

- No behavioral advertising
